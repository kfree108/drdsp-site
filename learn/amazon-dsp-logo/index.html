Which Amazon DSP do you mean?

Two completely different Amazons use the initials "DSP." One is the Delivery Service Partner program — the vans, the box logo on the side of a truck, the courier franchise Amazon runs in local markets. The other is the Demand-Side Platform — the programmatic system advertisers use to buy display, video and audio ads across Amazon and the open web. This page is about the second one, the ad-tech platform, because that's what "logo" searches from advertisers, agencies and brand marketers almost always turn out to mean once you ask what they're actually trying to do.

If you wanted the delivery van branding — for a franchise application, a vehicle wrap, a uniform — that's an Amazon Logistics asset entirely separate from anything below, and it isn't covered here.

Is there a downloadable Amazon DSP logo?

No, not in the way most people expect. Amazon doesn't publish a free "Amazon DSP" logo PNG for public download the way a SaaS company hands out a press kit. Amazon Ads maintains brand usage guidelines that govern how its name, wordmarks and logos can appear in anyone else's materials — case studies, agency decks, co-marketing pages — and using them without going through that approval path is a policy problem, not a design shortcut.

Most people landing on this search are trying to do one of three things: build a slide that says "we run Amazon DSP campaigns," badge a case study, or — more often — submit creative into an actual DSP campaign and assume Amazon wants its own logo somewhere in the ad. None of those actually need Amazon's logo. The first two need permission under Amazon Ads' brand guidelines. The third needs something else entirely: yours.

What Amazon DSP creative review actually asks for

If you're setting up a DSP campaign, the logo Amazon needs is your brand's, not its own, and it has to arrive in a specific format or creative review bounces it. The standard checklist: a vector logo (.ai or .eps, not a flattened PNG), high-resolution layered .PSD files for product or lifestyle imagery, background or key art matching the ad unit's dimensions, licensed fonts (.otf or .ttf), approved ad copy, and a working click-through URL or live ASIN.

Say a brand like HexClad is putting together its first DSP creative batch. The instinct is to grab the logo straight off the website — that's the file that fails. This trips up more accounts than it should. Across the 109 live Amazon DSP advertiser seats Full Circle and reMKTR run between them, a vector-format logo rejection is one of the most common reasons a first creative batch bounces back during onboarding — not a targeting problem, not a bid problem, just the wrong file type.

The common mistake, and how to fix a rejected asset

The mistake, plainly: someone grabs the logo off the brand's own website — usually a PNG or JPEG saved for web, 72 dpi, background baked in — and uploads that as the "logo" asset. It renders fine on a laptop screen and fails review because Amazon DSP needs a vector file it can scale cleanly across a banner, a Fire TV screen and a Kindle app without pixelating or showing a white box around what should be a transparent logo.

If review flags the asset, don't resubmit the same file at higher resolution — a bigger raster file is still raster. Get the vector original from whoever built the brand identity; most design agencies keep the .ai or .eps on file even if the brand team never asked for it. If nobody has the source file, that's the actual problem to solve before the campaign launch date, not a five-minute swap.

Common questions

Is the Amazon DSP logo the same as the Amazon Delivery Service Partner logo?

No. They're unrelated Amazon programs that happen to share an acronym. Delivery Service Partner is the courier franchise; the vans and driver branding belong to Amazon Logistics. Demand-Side Platform is the ad-buying system advertisers use for display, video and audio. This page covers the ad platform.

Can I use Amazon's logo in my agency's case study or pitch deck?

Only with permission under Amazon Ads' brand usage guidelines. There's no open license to badge your materials with Amazon's marks, and doing it without approval risks a takedown request rather than a free credibility boost.

What file format does Amazon DSP require for a brand logo in creative?

Vector — .ai or .eps — not a PNG or JPEG saved from a website. Amazon needs a file it can scale across banner sizes, connected TV screens and app placements without pixelating.

Why was my DSP creative rejected over the logo?

Almost always a format issue: a raster file submitted where a vector was required, low resolution, or a transparent logo that renders with a visible background box. Resubmitting the same file larger doesn't fix it — you need the vector source.

Where do I find Amazon's official brand guidelines for DSP creative?

Amazon publishes them alongside its ad dimensions and specifications documentation under "Amazon brand usage guidelines" and "creative acceptance policy" — check the current version on advertising.amazon.com before a campaign launch, since policy pages get updated without much notice.
