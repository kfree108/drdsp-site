What Retail Media Breakfast Club Is

Retail Media Breakfast Club is a newsletter and podcast published by industry commentator Kiri Masters, not a live event, not a trade show, and not anything run by Amazon. The name suggests a meetup; in practice it's a Monday-to-Thursday digest that lands around 6am ET, covering ad tech, retail media network (RMN) profiles, Amazon, AI, measurement debates, and industry outlook, plus a recurring lighter 'Funny' section.

The podcast episodes run roughly ten minutes and carry a sponsor credit, which is standard for trade media and worth knowing when you're weighing how a segment got framed. The written newsletter and podcast cover the same ground: commentary and analysis on what's happening in retail media, aimed at people already working in the space rather than newcomers needing definitions.

What It Covers, and Who It's For

The content splits into recognizable beats: RMN profiles (recent pieces have covered Dick's Sporting Goods, Sam's Club, and Chewy), measurement arguments (why ROAS 'refuses to die,' whether agentic buying collapses the ad tech stack), and AI coverage (what non-endemic brands are doing with Amazon's audience data, how shoppers misjudge AI-driven purchases). It suits people who want a fast, opinionated read on where the industry is heading — media buyers, RMN staff, agency strategists, anyone who needs talking points before a client call.

  • Ad Tech — stack debates, agentic buying, platform consolidation
  • RMN Profile — deep dives on individual retail media networks
  • Measurement — attribution debates and ROAS methodology arguments
  • AI — non-endemic advertisers, AI shopping behavior, agentic commerce

What it doesn't do is tell you anything about your own account. It's industry-level commentary, not a diagnostic for a specific advertiser's spend.

The Common Mistake: Treating Commentary as Evidence

The mistake we've watched clients make — and made ourselves before we tightened our own process — is reading an industry-wide observation and applying it directly to a single account. If a newsletter argues that ROAS is a flawed metric, or that AI shoppers overestimate what they bought, that's a real and useful signal about the industry. It is not evidence about whether your own display campaign on Amazon moved an incremental sale last month.

Last-click attribution inside Amazon's own reporting has the same problem at a smaller scale: it cannot prove incrementality and never could, because it credits whichever touchpoint happened last, regardless of what would have happened anyway. We used to lean on it more than we should have. The fix isn't a better dashboard — it's a holdout or matched control on the specific account, reconciled in Amazon Marketing Cloud so DSP and sponsored ads stop double-counting each other's touches.

When the Numbers Disagree With the Story

Sometimes the industry narrative and your own data point in opposite directions, and that's fine — it's the more common outcome. If the commentary says a channel is fading and your holdout shows real incremental lift, trust the holdout. If your holdout shows nothing, that's the answer too, and the right response is to stop, not to keep running the line item because it 'should' work in theory.

Every change we make carries three things before it runs: the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger. That third piece matters most when the news is bad — if a new audience segment or creative doesn't clear its own holdout after the agreed window, it comes out, not because the industry said so, but because the account said so.

Common questions

Is Retail Media Breakfast Club a live event or breakfast meetup?

No. Despite the name, it's a newsletter and podcast published Monday through Thursday, not an in-person gathering. There's no registration desk or venue — 'breakfast' refers to the early publishing time, around 6am ET.

Is Retail Media Breakfast Club affiliated with Amazon?

No. It's independent commentary produced by Kiri Masters and covers many retail media networks beyond Amazon, including Dick's Sporting Goods, Sam's Club, and Chewy, alongside Amazon-specific coverage.

Is it free to read?

The newsletter offers registration and the podcast is available on platforms like Apple Podcasts. Check the publisher's own site for current sign-up terms, since access details can change.

Can reading it replace measurement work on my own campaigns?

No. It's a useful way to stay current on industry arguments and RMN moves, but it can't tell you whether your own Amazon DSP or sponsored ads spend is incremental. That requires a holdout or matched control test on your specific account.
