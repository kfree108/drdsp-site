DSP or DSP — check which one you mean

Amazon uses "DSP" for two unrelated things. Delivery Service Partner is the van-fleet franchise program for people who want to run Amazon delivery routes — different site, different support line, nothing below applies to it. Amazon DSP, the Demand-Side Platform, is advertising software: it buys display, video and audio ad space programmatically, across Amazon-owned properties and off-Amazon inventory, using Amazon's shopping and streaming signals to pick who sees the ad.

If you typed "amazon dsp help" because a campaign isn't performing, or because the reporting doesn't add up, or because you're not sure whether you need it at all, this page is about that second one.

What Amazon DSP actually is and who runs it

DSP is not self-service in the way Sponsored Products is. You can run it two ways: through Amazon's own console if you have a trader who knows programmatic buying, or through Amazon's managed-service account team. Amazon's own advertising site states a minimum investment of USD 50,000 for that managed-service path with an Amazon account executive — that's their figure, on their page, and it's a floor, not a typical spend.

Either way, DSP buys inventory in auction, builds audiences from purchase and browse signals, and reports back impressions, clicks and — by default — last-click attributed sales. That last part is where most of the confusion starts, because last-click attribution was never built to answer the question people actually ask it: did this display ad cause an incremental sale, or would that sale have happened anyway from a search ad or organic browsing that ran alongside it?

The four places people actually get stuck

Almost every "DSP isn't working" question collapses into one of these:

  • Setup — line items, creative approval turnaround, and account structure that doesn't match the goal (awareness campaigns built like retargeting campaigns).
  • Targeting — remarketing pools too wide (spend wasted on people who'd have bought anyway) or too narrow (frequency capped into nothing).
  • Reporting that doesn't reconcile — DSP and Sponsored Products both claim credit for the same purchase, so the combined number looks better than either channel actually is.
  • Attribution — proving the display spend added sales rather than just riding along with search.

The fourth one is the real fix for the third. Reconciling in Amazon Marketing Cloud, where DSP and sponsored ads are joined at the shopper level instead of counted separately, is the only way to see what display actually contributed. Across 30 advertisers reconciled this way in July 2026, the book behind this page delivered 6.04x return on ad spend, 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM, a blended $1.42 cost-per-click, and a blended cost-per-acquisition of $5.49 across 57,137 attributed purchases — 20.1% of them new to the brand. That's what the numbers look like once double-counting is removed, not before.

When the fix doesn't work

Three situations, three different responses:

The number still looks wrong after you adjust it. Check the AMC join before you touch the campaign again — a lot of "DSP underperforming" tickets are actually a broken or stale data join, not a bidding problem.

The setting you're told to change is already on. Frequency caps and negative audience exclusions get set once and forgotten; before rebuilding an audience from scratch, pull the current config and confirm what's actually live versus what someone remembers setting.

You ran the fix and ROAS didn't move. That's a real result, not a failed test — it usually means the channel wasn't incremental in the first place. The next move is a holdout test (a matched group that sees no DSP ads) against the group that does, not more budget on the same targeting.

The mistake almost everyone makes — including us

The most common one: treating DSP like Sponsored Products and optimizing to last-click. It rewards placements that happen to sit right before a purchase, not the ones that actually moved the shopper. Turn off the wrong placement based on that number and you can cut something that was working.

The mistake we've made ourselves: turning on fully autonomous optimization before the measurement plan was solid enough to catch it if it went wrong. That's why every change we make now carries three things before it runs — the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger. It's a slower way to work. It's also the only way to know, after the fact, whether a change actually helped or just moved numbers around.

Self-service works if you already have someone who understands programmatic auctions and you have time to build the AMC reconciliation yourself — Amazon Ads Academy's free courses cover the console mechanics well.

Common questions

Is Amazon DSP the same as the Delivery Service Partner program?

No. Delivery Service Partner is Amazon's courier franchise for running delivery vans. Amazon DSP, the Demand-Side Platform, is advertising software for buying display, video and audio ads. Same three letters, nothing else in common.

Why don't my DSP and Sponsored Products numbers add up?

Both channels can claim credit for the same purchase when they're reported separately, which inflates the combined total. Reconciling the two in Amazon Marketing Cloud, at the shopper level, removes the double-counting and shows what each channel actually contributed.

What's the minimum budget for Amazon DSP?

Amazon's own advertising site lists a USD 50,000 minimum investment for its managed-service path with an Amazon account executive. Self-service DSP through the console doesn't carry that same published floor, but it does require someone who can run a programmatic auction. Managed partners typically quote pricing against your actual budget and scope rather than publishing a fixed number.

Do I need an agency to run Amazon DSP well?

Not necessarily. If you have in-house programmatic expertise and time to build AMC reconciliation, self-service can work. Most teams that come looking for "DSP help" are looking for that reconciliation and incrementality testing specifically, which is the part a managed partner typically builds faster because they've already built it elsewhere.

How do I know if my DSP campaigns are actually working, not just running?

Last-click attribution alone won't tell you. Run a holdout test — a matched group that doesn't see the ads compared against one that does — or reconcile DSP against sponsored ads in Amazon Marketing Cloud. If ROAS doesn't hold up under either check, the spend likely wasn't incremental.
