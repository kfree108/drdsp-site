Google Brand Lift: What It Actually Measures, and What It Can't

The short answer

Google Brand Lift is a free YouTube survey tool that compares people who saw your video ad against a matched group who didn't, to measure shifts in ad recall, awareness, consideration and purchase intent. It requires a Google account rep, a minimum spend, and only works on video, audio or Demand Gen campaigns.

What Google Brand Lift Actually Measures

Google Brand Lift runs a one-question survey on YouTube after someone watches, or would have watched, your video, audio or Demand Gen ad. It shows the same question to a randomized exposed group who saw the ad and a control group who were eligible to see it but didn't. The gap between the two groups' answers is the lift.

The metrics on offer are ad recall, brand awareness, consideration, favorability and purchase intent — you pick up to three per study, plus one custom metric. None of these are purchase data. They're self-reported opinion, collected at scale, about whether the ad changed how someone feels or what they say they intend to do next.

What You Need Before You Can Run One

Brand Lift isn't a self-serve toggle. You need a Google account representative attached to your account — without one, the option doesn't appear no matter how the account is configured. You also need an active Video, audio, or Demand Gen campaign; Demand Gen studies only capture YouTube video placements, not Gmail or Discover inventory, so a Demand Gen budget spread heavily across those surfaces will under-report.

  • A minimum spend threshold per country, which needs to be met inside a 10-day measurement window
  • Survey approval, which can take up to 48 hours and can be rejected for policy or quality issues
  • Up to three competitor product or brand names entered as survey answer options
  • A study strength setting — Standard, Lift, or Enhanced — that trades speed against statistical confidence

Miss the budget window and the study reverts to "Not Eligible," even if you fix the underlying campaign a few hours later. The eligibility calculator updates near-live, but survey issuance lags it by hours, so a study can look eligible and still not be collecting responses yet.

Reading the Numbers: A Worked Example

The dashboard hands you raw survey figures: baseline positive response rate, exposed positive response rate, and the two survey volumes behind them. Here's the arithmetic underneath, using round numbers purely to show the mechanics, not a real campaign's result:

Say the baseline group answers "yes, I recall this ad" 8% of the time, and the exposed group answers yes 14% of the time. That's an absolute lift of 6 percentage points and a relative lift of 75% (6 divided by 8). If 500,000 people were exposed, roughly 30,000 of them get counted as "lifted." Divide media spend by that number and you get cost per lifted user — a figure that looks cheap or expensive almost entirely based on how big the denominator is, not how good the creative was.

When the Study Comes Back Bad, Confusing, or 'Not Eligible'

Three things go wrong more often than the help docs let on.

  • Budget met too slowly. If spend trickles in over the 10-day window instead of hitting the requirement early, response volume stays too thin to read anything by the deadline.
  • Mixed formats without a plan. Adding an audio campaign to a video-only Ad Recall study is blocked outright; running both from day one avoids the block but also splits the sample across two different ad experiences, which softens the read on either one.
  • Too many metrics on too little traffic. Selecting three metrics against a modest budget spreads survey responses three ways. Each individual lift number ends up sitting inside the noise.

We've made a version of this mistake ourselves, on the display side rather than YouTube: running a test with three creative variants under one measurement plan, then getting a result too thin to act on because the audience got split three ways before it ever hit statistical power. The fix isn't a different platform. It's fewer variables per test, and a plan for how big the exposed group needs to be before the test starts.

Brand Lift Tells You Perception Shifted, Not Whether It Sold Anything

Brand Lift answers a narrower question than most people assume: did stated perception move. It doesn't reconcile against what someone actually bought, and it can't see whether the same person was also served a search ad, a display ad, or nothing at all outside YouTube. Two campaigns can show identical lift numbers and produce very different sales — the survey has no way to tell you which.

That's the same problem retail media measurement runs into, and it gets solved differently there: instead of asking people what they think, matched control groups and clean-room reconciliation look at what they actually purchased. On Amazon, that means comparing exposed and holdout groups' real purchase records inside Amazon Marketing Cloud, where display and sponsored ads stop double-counting each other. Across 30 advertisers we manage in July 2026, that kind of reconciliation turned 78.4 million impressions and a $4.00 CPM into 57,137 attributed purchases at a blended $5.49 cost per acquisition, 20.1% of them new to the brand — numbers pulled from purchase records, not a survey question.

Common questions

Is Google Brand Lift free?

The tool itself carries no separate fee, but it isn't free to run: you need a Google account representative to enable it, and enough media spend to hit the per-country budget requirement inside a 10-day window. No budget, no eligible study.

Why does my Brand Lift study say 'Not Eligible'?

Usually because the budget requirement hasn't been met yet inside the measurement window, or a recent campaign change — a pause, an end date, a budget cut — hasn't propagated through the eligibility calculator yet, which can take a few hours. If a campaign served a handful of impressions before you paused it and barely gathered survey responses, the better move is to stop that study and start a fresh one rather than try to rescue it.

What's the difference between Standard, Lift, and Enhanced Lift?

They're strength settings that trade speed for statistical confidence, not different metrics. A stronger setting needs a larger sample before it reports numbers, which usually means either more budget or more time to reach it.

Does Brand Lift tell me if the campaign drove sales?

No. It measures self-reported recall, awareness, consideration, favorability or purchase intent — attitudes, not transactions. If you need to know whether an ad caused an actual purchase, that's a different exercise: a holdout or matched-control test measured against purchase records, not a survey.

Can I run Brand Lift on Search or Shopping campaigns?

No. It's built for Video, audio, and Demand Gen campaigns running on YouTube. Demand Gen studies specifically only capture the YouTube video placements within that campaign, not Gmail or Discover inventory.
