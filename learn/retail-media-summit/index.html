What the Retail Media Summit actually is (and isn't)

The name covers three real things and one that doesn't exist. The flagship Retail Media Summit is run by the Path to Purchase Institute (P2PI, part of EnsembleIQ) and lands at the Palmer House Hilton in Chicago — 2026 was its fifth year, running June 2–4. A separate one-day event, Retail Media Summit Canada, has run in Toronto for four years running. Both are CPG-and-retailer conferences about retail media strategy across every network — Amazon, Walmart Connect, Kroger Precision Marketing, Instacart, Albertsons Media Collective — not a single vendor's show.

There's no event called "Douglas Retail Media Summit." The closest match in the actual 2026 speaker lineup is Doug Chavez, EVP of Global Retail Channel Strategy at Mars United Commerce, who spoke in Chicago. If that's what brought you here, you're likely after his session or his firm's work, not a separate conference.

Neither event's own page publishes ticket pricing in what we reviewed — check events.p2pi.com directly for current tiers and any early-bird cutoff before you budget for it.

Who's in the room, and why it matters for planning

The organizer's own attendee data is the most useful part of the listing: one in four attendees carries a C-suite, SVP or GM title, and over two-thirds hold Director level or higher. That's not filler — it means the sessions are pitched at people who set budget, not people who execute it. If you're two levels down from your VP, you'll come home with strategy you then have to translate into a brief.

The speaker list leans retailer and holding-company: Walmart Connect, Kroger Precision Marketing, Instacart, Albertsons Media Collective, plus agency and measurement voices. That mix is the real value — it's where a retailer's ad-sales lead and a CPG's shopper marketing director end up in the same room. It's weaker on execution detail: nobody walks off that stage and hands you a measurement plan.

The gap the summit doesn't cover: is the number you heard actually real

Here's the part every recap article skips: what do you do with a number someone says on stage? Say a retailer rep claims their network drove a 4x return. That figure is almost always last-click or self-reported — it tells you a purchase happened after exposure, not that the exposure caused it. Last-click attribution can't answer that question and never could; you need a holdout group or a matched control that didn't see the ad.

We run that test on our own Amazon DSP book, so the shape of the number is worth showing rather than describing. Across 30 advertisers in July 2026, holdout-tested activity delivered a 6.04x return on ad spend, 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM, and a blended $1.42 cost-per-click. Blended cost per acquisition landed at $5.49 across 57,137 attributed purchases — 20.1% of them new to the brand. That number only means something because it's reconciled in Amazon Marketing Cloud, where DSP and sponsored ads stop double-counting the same purchase. A stage claim without that reconciliation step is a story, not a result.

The common mistake: taking a stage claim home as a strategy

The most common mistake we see brands make after a summit — one we've made ourselves early in a client relationship — is copying a tactic that worked at a much bigger advertiser's budget and expecting it to scale down linearly. A 78-million-impression campaign behaves differently at a tenth of that spend: CPMs, frequency caps and audience overlap all move. Hearing a tactic praised on stage tells you it's worth testing, not that it's worth committing to before you've run it at your own scale with a rollback point defined.

The second mistake is treating a vendor's booth pitch the same as a peer's case-study talk. A vendor is selling; a practitioner sharing a number in a panel usually isn't. Ask which one you're hearing before you act on it.

If the number you brought back doesn't hold up

If you bring a tactic home and the lift doesn't show up, the fix isn't to keep running it hoping it catches up, and it isn't to kill it after one week either. Set the rollback trigger before you launch — a specific number, by a specific date, that means stop. We attach three things to every change before it runs: the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and that rollback trigger. If the tactic came from a stage talk with no measurement plan attached, build one before you spend against it, not after.

Which one you should actually pick

The Chicago flagship suits U.S. CPG and retailer teams setting annual retail media strategy and wanting the widest speaker mix. The Toronto edition suits teams whose budget and partners are Canada-specific. If you searched for "Douglas," you likely want Doug Chavez's session or Mars United Commerce's work, not a separate event.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

When is the next Retail Media Summit?

The 2026 Chicago edition closed with a "see you in 2027" message and no new date published at time of writing. Check events.p2pi.com for the release. The Canada edition in Toronto runs on its own separate annual cycle.

Is the EnsembleIQ event listing the same as the P2PI event page?

Yes. EnsembleIQ owns the Path to Purchase Institute, which runs the summit. The EnsembleIQ calendar entry and the P2PI event page both describe the same Chicago event at the Palmer House Hilton.

Does the summit cover Amazon Advertising specifically?

No single network. Speakers and sponsors span Walmart Connect, Kroger Precision Marketing, Instacart, Albertsons Media Collective, and Amazon-adjacent agencies — it's a cross-retailer strategy conference, not an Amazon-only event.

What does a ticket cost?

Pricing isn't published on the pages we reviewed. Check P2PI's own site for current tiers, including any early-bird or hosted-buyer program rates, before you budget for it.

What is the "Douglas Retail Media Summit"?

There's no conference by that name. The most likely match is Doug Chavez, EVP of Global Retail Channel Strategy at Mars United Commerce, who spoke at the 2026 Chicago summit — or it's a mis-remembered search for P2PI's Retail Media Summit itself.
