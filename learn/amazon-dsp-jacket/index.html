DSP can also mean Demand-Side Platform, Amazon's ad-buying system, which has nothing to do with clothing.

What "DSP" means in this search

Type "amazon dsp jacket" and you get eBay listings for bomber jackets from AWS re:Invent, secondhand Amazon Essentials coats, and a handful of items tagged "DSP" with no consistent brand or style. That scatter is the tell: there's no single official product behind this search, because DSP means two unrelated things at Amazon, and the acronym collision is the whole story.

One DSP is Delivery Service Partner — the network of independently owned local courier companies that deliver Amazon packages, whose drivers wear a branded uniform kit. The other is Demand-Side Platform, Amazon's programmatic system for buying display, video and audio ads. Same three letters, same parent company, nothing else in common.

DSP driver jackets: a uniform, not a product

The jacket most people searching this term actually want is the one worn by Delivery Service Partner drivers. It's issued by the DSP operator — the local business that owns the delivery contract and employs the driver — as part of a standard uniform package. Amazon doesn't sell it to the public, which is exactly why the only place it shows up is resale marketplaces.

  • Genuine driver uniform pieces — decommissioned stock, employee overstock, or items sold on by former drivers
  • AWS conference swag — the bomber jackets given out at re:Invent, which have nothing to do with delivery at all
  • Amazon Essentials retail jackets — ordinary clothing that happens to say "Amazon" on the label
  • Third-party reproductions — jackets embroidered with an Amazon-style logo by a seller, not issued by Amazon or a DSP operator

If you're an active DSP driver and need one, the right move is asking your operator's uniform contact, not buying secondhand. If you're not a driver, there isn't a legitimate retail path to one — it's a work asset tied to an employment relationship, not merchandise.

The other DSP: Amazon's Demand-Side Platform

Demand-Side Platform is how brands buy programmatic ads on and off Amazon — display, streaming video, audio — targeted using Amazon's shopping and browsing data. It sits next to Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands, but it's a separate buy, with separate reporting, and it's where a lot of the confusion about "what DSP actually does" starts.

Here's what real DSP performance looks like, not a rounded press-release number: across 30 advertisers running Amazon DSP in July 2026, the book delivered 6.04x return on ad spend, 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM and a blended $1.42 cost-per-click. Blended cost per acquisition was $5.49 across 57,137 attributed purchases, 20.1% of them from shoppers new to the brand.

The harder question is whether those purchases would have happened anyway. Last-click attribution hands DSP credit for any sale that happened after an impression, even if a Sponsored Products click closed it minutes later. That's not a DSP problem specifically — it's a measurement problem, and the only honest fix is reconciling DSP and sponsored ads together in Amazon Marketing Cloud, or running a holdout group so you can see what happens when DSP is off.

The common mistake — including one we've made

The mistake we see most, and one we've made ourselves early in a launch: turning on full-funnel DSP retargeting without holding out a control cell first. The reported "lift" was really remarketing to the same warm audience Sponsored Products was already converting — DSP wasn't adding reach, it was double-billing for a sale that was going to happen anyway.

The fix isn't turning DSP off. It's building the test before you scale the spend: a matched holdout group, a defined measurement window, and a rollback trigger if the number doesn't hold up. Brands running retargeting at any real scale — a kitchenware brand like HexClad or a haircare line like BK Beauty, for instance — need to know whether DSP is winning back a shopper who'd already decided, or actually pulling in someone new.

When the answer disappoints

If you came here looking for the driver jacket and there isn't one to buy: that's the correct answer, not a dead end in the research. Go to your DSP operator's HR or uniform contact. Buying a secondhand or reproduction jacket and wearing it as if you're affiliated with Amazon isn't something we'd advise, regardless of where it's sold.

If you came here as an advertiser and your DSP number is bad — ROAS looks weak, CPA is climbing — don't kill the channel off a last-click report. Reconcile in AMC first to see what DSP is actually adding on top of sponsored ads, and check whether a holdout group exists at all. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the recurring lesson is that a weak-looking DSP number is more often a measurement gap than a real performance problem.

Common questions

Is there an official Amazon DSP jacket you can buy?

No. The jacket associated with "DSP" is a Delivery Service Partner uniform piece, issued by the courier operator that employs the driver. It isn't sold to the public. What shows up on marketplaces is a mix of secondhand uniform stock, unrelated AWS conference swag, and generic Amazon-branded retail clothing.

Does DSP always mean Delivery Service Partner at Amazon?

No — it's one of two unrelated meanings. Delivery Service Partner is the courier network. Demand-Side Platform is Amazon's programmatic ad-buying system. Which one someone means depends entirely on context; there's no shared meaning between the two.

Why do eBay listings for "Amazon DSP jacket" show random branded gear?

Because sellers tag anything Amazon-adjacent with the search terms buyers use, whether or not it's related. AWS conference bomber jackets, Amazon Essentials retail jackets, and genuine driver uniform pieces all end up under the same query even though they came from completely different parts of the company.

How is Amazon's Demand-Side Platform different from Sponsored Ads?

Sponsored Ads run inside Amazon's search and product pages on a cost-per-click model. DSP buys display, video and audio placements on and off Amazon, often on an impression basis, and is typically used for retargeting and reach rather than bottom-of-funnel clicks. The two get reported separately, which is exactly why double counting is easy to miss.

How do I know if my Amazon DSP campaign is actually working?

Check it against a holdout group or reconcile it in Amazon Marketing Cloud alongside sponsored ads. Across 30 advertisers in July 2026 the book returned 6.04x ROAS and a $5.49 blended CPA — but that figure only means something once you know it isn't just re-crediting sales sponsored ads already closed.
