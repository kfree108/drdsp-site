What H-E-B Retail Media Actually Sells

H-E-B Retail Media (HRM) is the in-house ad network built on top of H-E-B's grocery footprint — over 455 locations and 300-plus communities across Texas, per H-E-B's own account. It sells three layers of inventory. In-store: shelf blades, digital service screens, fuel pump screens. Onsite: homepage carousels, shoppable recipes, digital end caps and category aisle banners on heb.com and the My H-E-B App. Offsite: programmatic display and social placements that extend reach beyond H-E-B's own properties.

That's a full-funnel stack for a single-market grocer, which is unusual. Most regional grocery retail media networks stop at onsite banners and email. H-E-B has built out physical store media too, which matters if your brand's win condition is shelf-adjacent visibility at the moment someone is standing in the aisle, not just a click on a website.

How Brands Actually Buy Into It

There's no public rate card. H-E-B's own hiring posts reference an Account Executive III role and a push toward "self-service capabilities," which suggests a managed, rep-led buy today with some self-serve tooling arriving. If you want specifics — minimum spend, CPMs, whether shelf placement is guaranteed or auction-based — you get those on a call with an account exec, not from the website.

H-E-B also cites a 97% overlap between its eCommerce shoppers and in-store shoppers, framed as "omnichannel loyalty." That's a real and useful fact about audience reach. It is not, on its own, evidence that an ad campaign caused incremental sales — it just says the same people shop both channels, which is true of most grocery chains with an app.

The Worked Example: What 'Proven Results' Actually Needs to Show

H-E-B's page claims campaigns have "demonstrated significant sales lifts," without publishing the methodology. That's the gap worth naming plainly, not filling in for them: a sales lift number reported inside a retailer's own dashboard is almost always last-click or exposure-based attribution. It tells you a purchase happened after someone saw an ad. It does not tell you the purchase wouldn't have happened anyway.

The only way to answer that is a holdout: hold a matched set of stores or households out of the campaign, run the same period, compare. If H-E-B (or any retail media network) can't produce that comparison, the honest read is that the lift number is directional, not causal — useful for a trend line, not for a budget decision.

We run this discipline on the Amazon DSP side, reconciled in Amazon Marketing Cloud so display and sponsored ads stop double-counting each other. Across 30 advertisers in July 2026, that approach delivered a 6.04x return on ad spend, 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM and a blended $1.42 cost-per-click, with a blended cost-per-acquisition of $5.49 across 57,137 attributed purchases, 20.1% of them from shoppers new to the brand. Different retailer, same principle: the number only means something once you know what it's being compared against.

Common Mistakes — Including Ones We've Made

  • Treating a vendor-reported lift as proof. If the number comes from the retailer's own dashboard and there's no holdout, it's a signal, not a result.
  • Double-counting across channels. A brand running H-E-B in-store media and Amazon Sponsored Products on the same SKU can end up crediting the same shopper twice — once in each walled garden.
  • Buying reach without confirming shelf reality. A national media plan that includes Texas as a line item doesn't mean the SKU is actually stocked or endcapped at the H-E-B locations the campaign is targeting.
  • We've made a version of this mistake too. Early DSP builds we've run have shipped midfunnel display against a broad audience with no measurement plan attached, and called a directional signal a result. That's exactly the gap a holdout and a defined rollback trigger are meant to close before a change goes live, not after.

What to Do When the Number Looks Wrong

If a reported lift doesn't hold up, or a campaign's ROAS looks off against the media spend, don't renew on faith. Ask the rep for the attribution window and whether a holdout or matched-market test exists. If one doesn't, treat the reported number as directional and build your own control — even a rough one, a few comparable stores or a comparable ZIP set held out of the buy for one cycle.

If the fix genuinely didn't work — the lift doesn't replicate, the audience overlap doesn't convert — the right move is to stop the spend, not to keep running it because it's already budgeted. That's true whether the media is bought from H-E-B, from Amazon, or from anyone else selling a walled-garden dashboard.

Common questions

Is H-E-B Retail Media the same as Amazon retail media?

No. H-E-B Retail Media is a grocery retailer's in-house network covering its own stores, app, and website in Texas. Amazon's retail media includes Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Amazon DSP, which runs across Amazon's marketplace and off-Amazon inventory nationally. A brand selling into H-E-B stores and on Amazon will likely need both, and should watch for the same shopper getting counted twice across the two.

Does H-E-B Retail Media publish self-serve pricing?

Not publicly as of the source material available. H-E-B's own hiring posts reference expanding "self-service capabilities," which implies the buying model is evolving, but no rate card or self-serve pricing structure is published on their site. Specifics come from an account executive on a call.

Who genuinely fits H-E-B Retail Media best?

CPG brands with real distribution inside H-E-B stores and a reason to care specifically about Texas — local or regional brands, or national brands treating Texas as a priority market. It suits brands wanting shelf-adjacent, in-store visibility as much as digital reach. It doesn't help a brand with no H-E-B shelf presence, or one whose growth priority is outside Texas.

What does the 97% omnichannel overlap figure actually prove?

It shows that most H-E-B eCommerce shoppers also shop in-store — a real and useful audience fact for planning cross-channel reach. It doesn't prove that an ad campaign caused an incremental sale. Overlap is a targeting fact, not a causality fact; those require different evidence.

Can I run H-E-B Retail Media and Amazon ads on the same SKU without double-counting results?

Not automatically. Each platform reports attribution inside its own walled garden, so a shopper exposed to both can get credited to both. On the Amazon side, reconciling Sponsored Ads and DSP in Amazon Marketing Cloud is the standard fix. There's no equivalent cross-retailer reconciliation tool for H-E-B and Amazon together — that comparison has to be built manually, with a holdout, if you need it.
