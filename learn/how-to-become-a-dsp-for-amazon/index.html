Two Unrelated Amazon DSPs — Pick the Right One First

"DSP" means two unrelated things once Amazon is involved, and mixing them up wastes your afternoon. A Delivery Service Partner is a courier franchise: you'd own vans, hire drivers, and deliver Amazon packages under contract. If that's what brought you here, Amazon's own driver-hiring and DSP-partner sites cover the driver and franchise-owner path directly — this page is not that.

The other DSP is Amazon's Demand-Side Platform — the system brands and agencies use to buy programmatic display, video and audio ads across Amazon-owned properties and third-party inventory Amazon has access to. "Becoming a DSP" in this sense means getting an account, a seat, or an agency relationship that lets you buy through it. That's what the rest of this page walks through.

The Three Real Routes Into Amazon's Advertising DSP

There is no single sign-up button that turns any seller into a DSP advertiser. Access comes through one of three routes, and each one changes who controls the account and who is accountable when something underperforms.

  • Self-service, direct with Amazon. You get an Amazon Advertising account rep, commit to a spend level, and your own team builds and runs campaigns inside the DSP console. Amazon sets the eligibility bar and it has shifted over the years, so check current requirements rather than trust a number from an old blog post.
  • Through an agency that already holds DSP seats. Most agencies with Amazon experience have existing seats and can add your brand under theirs. You skip the direct eligibility conversation with Amazon, but you inherit however that agency measures and reports — which varies a lot.
  • Through a managed product. A smaller group of operators run DSP as a packaged service: strategy, execution and measurement bundled, with the client choosing how much control to keep. This is the category Dr. DSP sits in, and it's the one most people searching this phrase haven't been told exists.

Whichever route, the mechanics underneath are the same: you need Amazon Ads account access, ideally Brand Registry if you're selling products, and a way to connect DSP activity to your sales data — increasingly through Amazon Marketing Cloud, since DSP and Sponsored Ads reporting overlap and double-count each other if left unreconciled.

That's the part almost nobody explains when they answer "how do I become a DSP advertiser": the access is a formality, the measurement is the actual project.

The Mistake Almost Everyone Makes Once They're Live

The most common failure isn't picking the wrong access route. It's turning DSP on, watching last-click ROAS inside the console, and either declaring victory or pulling the plug — both based on a number the platform cannot actually prove.

Last-click attribution assigns credit to whichever ad a shopper touched last, regardless of whether they were going to buy anyway. Display and video ads sit high in the funnel; they get under-credited by that logic almost every time. A brand can be running DSP that's genuinely adding incremental sales and still see a discouraging number, or running DSP that's adding nothing and see a flattering one, because Sponsored Ads picked up the same shopper on the way out.

The fix is a holdout or matched-control test: hold a portion of the audience back from DSP exposure, compare their purchase rate to the exposed group, and read the difference in AMC rather than the console. If the fix doesn't move the number — if a holdout shows no lift after a full measurement cycle — that's the honest signal to cut spend, not a reason to keep running the same campaign hoping attribution catches up. Every change worth making should carry three things before it runs: the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger for if it doesn't work.

Common questions

Do I need Amazon Brand Registry to use the DSP?

Not strictly for DSP access itself, but Brand Registry gives you the sales and catalog connections most measurement setups rely on. If you're a seller running DSP without it, you'll have a harder time tying impressions back to purchases.

What's the minimum ad spend to access Amazon DSP directly?

Amazon doesn't publish a fixed figure that stays true for long, and it has changed over time, so check current requirements directly with an Amazon rep rather than trust a cited number. Agencies and managed products often have lower or no direct minimum because they aggregate spend across accounts.

Is Amazon DSP the same as Sponsored Display?

No. Sponsored Display is auction-based, self-serve, and available without an account minimum. DSP is programmatic, reaches broader inventory including off-Amazon placements, and typically requires one of the three access routes described above.

I searched this looking to drive for Amazon, not buy ads — where do I go?

You want a Delivery Service Partner, which is a separate courier franchise business — you'd own the vans and hire drivers, or apply to drive for one. Amazon's own hiring pages cover that path; it has nothing to do with the advertising platform this page describes.

Can I run Amazon DSP without an agency or managed partner?

Yes, if you meet Amazon's self-service eligibility. The harder part isn't the account — it's building the Amazon Marketing Cloud measurement work yourself, which takes a real analytics resource most in-house teams don't have spare.
