The short answer

No single "Amazon DSP number" exists because DSP means two unrelated things at Amazon: the advertising Demand-Side Platform and the Delivery Service Partner courier program. This page covers the advertising DSP in depth and points you to the right contact for the delivery one.

Which Amazon DSP do you mean?

Amazon DSP is Amazon's programmatic buying platform for display, video and audio ads — the thing brands use to run ads off Amazon.com, on Fire TV, on Twitch, across the open web, bought through Amazon's ad stack. That's what this page is about, because it's what we run.

DSP as in Delivery Service Partner is a completely different thing: independent local courier businesses that Amazon contracts with to deliver packages. If you searched for a driving job, a lost package, or an "Amazon DSP offboarding number" for a delivery role you're leaving, you're in the second bucket, and the answer looks nothing like the first one.

Neither one has a single published phone number the way FedEx or UPS does. That's not an oversight — it's structural, and the reasons are different for each, which is why we cover both below instead of pretending the search only means one thing.

Amazon Advertising DSP: how support actually works

There is no public hotline for Amazon Advertising DSP. Support runs through three channels depending on how you access the platform: self-service accounts get a ticketing system inside the console, larger spenders get an assigned Amazon account team, and agencies or managed-service partners route issues through their own relationship with Amazon on your behalf.

At scale, "support" stops meaning a phone call at all. Full Circle and reMKTR operate 109 live Amazon DSP advertiser seats between them — at that volume, the work isn't answering tickets, it's account teams checking pacing, creative delivery, deal IDs and audience overlap before something breaks, and reconciling spend against Amazon Marketing Cloud so display and sponsored ads stop double-counting each other's credit.

If you're a brand running DSP in-house, the practical version of "support" is: know your Amazon Ads rep, use the console's help center for platform bugs, and if you're managing real budget, get a second set of eyes on measurement — because the platform will tell you a number went up. It won't tell you whether that number is real.

Delivery Service Partner: drivers, offboarding, and who actually answers

Each Delivery Service Partner is an independent small business. Amazon contracts with them for delivery capacity, but hiring, scheduling, pay, benefits and termination are the DSP's responsibility, not Amazon's. That's the direct answer to "Amazon DSP offboarding number": there isn't one, because Amazon doesn't run offboarding for DSP employees. Final paycheck questions, badge and uniform returns, and separation paperwork go through the specific DSP company that employed you — usually its site HR contact or ops manager, the same people who onboarded you.

If your question is instead about a package — it's late, it shows delivered but you can't find it, you need to change an address — that goes to the carrier listed on your order (Amazon's own carrier contact page lists these), not to a DSP directly and not to Amazon advertising support.

The confusion is understandable. Amazon uses "DSP" for both a driving job and a media-buying platform, and search engines mix the results. But the two never share a contact path, a support team, or a phone tree, because they're run by entirely different parts of the business.

When the number you have doesn't work

The most common mistake is matching the issue to the wrong owner. Someone leaving a DSP driving job calls Amazon customer service and gets bounced, because Amazon customer service handles orders, not DSP employment. An advertiser emails a generic Amazon Ads inbox looking for a person and gets a bot, because DSP support at scale is built around account teams and tickets, not inbound calls.

We've made the equivalent mistake on the advertising side ourselves: treating a dashboard number as an answer instead of a starting point. Last-click attribution will confidently report a return on ad spend. It can't tell you whether that spend caused a sale or just got credit for one that was going to happen anyway. Across 30 advertisers in our own book in July 2026, the reported numbers were 6.04x return on ad spend, 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM, a blended $1.42 cost-per-click, and a $5.49 cost per acquisition across 57,137 attributed purchases — 20.1% of them new-to-brand. Those figures are only worth trusting because they were checked against holdouts and matched controls, not because the platform reported them.

So the fix, in both cases, is the same shape: don't call the number you assume is right — find the party that actually owns the issue, whether that's a carrier, a DSP employer, or a measurement plan instead of a dashboard.

Common questions

Is there a phone number for Amazon DSP advertising support?

No public one. Support runs through the console's ticketing system for self-service accounts, an assigned Amazon account team for larger spenders, or an agency or managed-service partner acting on your behalf. At real scale, support looks like account teams checking pacing and measurement, not a call center.

What is the Amazon DSP offboarding number?

There isn't one run by Amazon. Delivery Service Partners are independent businesses responsible for their own hiring and termination, so offboarding — final pay, returning a badge or uniform — goes through the specific DSP company that employed you, not Amazon corporate.

How do I contact Amazon about a delivery driver problem?

If it's about a package, use the carrier listed on your order via Amazon's carrier contact page. If it's about the driver's employment — pay, hours, a job complaint — that goes to the Delivery Service Partner business that employs the driver, since Amazon doesn't manage DSP staff directly.

Why doesn't Amazon Ads publish a support phone number for DSP?

Because the product is built around account relationships and console tooling rather than inbound calls. Self-service advertisers get ticketing; managed advertisers get a named team. It scales more consistently than a phone tree once you're running seats in the dozens or hundreds, which is closer to how larger books like Full Circle's 109 live DSP seats actually operate.

Is Amazon DSP the advertising platform the same as the Amazon DSP delivery program?

No. They share three letters and nothing else. One is Amazon's programmatic ad-buying platform for display, video and audio. The other is a network of independent courier businesses that deliver packages. Different teams, different contacts, different everything — the overlap in the name is the only thing they have in common.
