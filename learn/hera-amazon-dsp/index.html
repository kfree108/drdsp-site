Two things named "DSP," and Hera is only one of them

"DSP" means two completely different things inside the Amazon ecosystem, and the overlap causes real confusion. A Delivery Service Partner is the franchise-style last-mile courier business Amazon set up in 2018 — independent owners running vans and drivers under Amazon's brand. A Demand-Side Platform is unrelated: it's Amazon's programmatic system for buying display, video and audio ads, used by brands that sell products on Amazon, not by delivery contractors.

Hera Solutions, Inc. (Atlanta, founded 2020) builds software for the first group. It is explicit on its own site that it is not affiliated with Amazon or its subsidiaries — it's a third-party tool sold to DSP owners, the same way a scheduling app is sold to any small trucking company. If you searched "hera amazon dsp" because you're comparing operations software for your delivery fleet, you're in the right neighborhood with Hera. If you searched it because you sell a product on Amazon and someone mentioned "DSP" in a media plan, you're looking for the ad platform, and Hera does not do that job at all.

What Hera actually does, stated fairly

Hera is built to replace the spreadsheets and text threads that most small DSPs run on. Its published feature set covers staff scheduling and rostering synced to Amazon and ADP, a single company phone number for all driver communication, AI-evaluated vehicle photo checks, performance tracking pulled from Amazon Scorecards, inventory management for uniforms and devices, e-signatures with an audit trail, and automated SMS coaching tied to driver metrics.

That's a genuinely useful bundle for a DSP owner drowning in OSHA reports, Netradyne scores, and weekly bonus tracking. It's a small team — Hera lists four employees on LinkedIn — selling a subscription product with a trial period; it does not publish a price on its site, so if you're evaluating it, expect to get a quote rather than a rate card. None of this touches advertising, ad spend, or Amazon's ad console in any way.

What Amazon DSP — the ad platform — actually is, with real numbers

Amazon DSP lets advertisers buy display, video and audio placements programmatically, on and off Amazon, targeting shoppers by behavior rather than keyword. The number that gets quoted most — return on ad spend from last-click attribution — cannot prove that the display or video actually caused the sale. Amazon's last-click system credits whichever ad a shopper touched last, even if they were already headed to buy. Proving incrementality requires a holdout: running the campaign against one matched group and withholding it from another, then comparing the difference, reconciled in Amazon Marketing Cloud so DSP and sponsored ads stop double-counting the same shopper.

Here's what that looks like on real media. Across 30 advertisers running Amazon DSP in July 2026, the book returned 6.04x ROAS on 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM, with a blended $1.42 CPC. That translated to 57,137 attributed purchases at a blended $5.49 CPA, with 20.1% new-to-brand. Read those numbers as a chain: CPM tells you what reach costs, CPC tells you what an engaged click costs, CPA tells you what an acquisition costs, and the new-to-brand share tells you how much of that acquisition was actually incremental rather than shoppers who'd have converted anyway.

How to tell which product you actually need

  • You run vans, drivers and routes for Amazon as a DSP owner: you need operations software like Hera — scheduling, compliance, coaching, fleet tracking.
  • You sell a product on Amazon and want to buy display, video or audio ads: you need Amazon DSP, the ad platform — either self-serve through Amazon, through an agency, or run as a managed product.
  • You're not sure which: ask whether the tool touches drivers and vehicles, or impressions and ad spend. There's no product that does both, and nothing on the market currently bridges the two.

The mistake advertisers make with DSP numbers — and what to do when the fix doesn't work

The common mistake on the advertising side is trusting the attributed ROAS on its own. A campaign can show a strong last-click number and still add nothing, because Amazon's attribution window is generous enough to credit shoppers who were already converting through search or an existing brand relationship. We've made this mistake ourselves early in a managed book — reading a good weekly ROAS as proof the tactic was working, before running it against a holdout and finding the lift wasn't there.

When a holdout or matched-control test comes back flat, the right move isn't to cut the budget and move on quietly. It's to check what changed: placement mix, frequency caps, and whether the DSP audience overlaps with a sponsored ads audience already converting the same shoppers in AMC. Every change should carry a measurement plan and a rollback trigger before it runs, so a bad test doesn't get buried — it gets diagnosed.

Common questions

Is Hera affiliated with Amazon?

No. Hera Solutions, Inc. states plainly on its own site that it is not affiliated with Amazon or its subsidiaries. It's a third-party operations tool sold to Amazon Delivery Service Partner owners.

Can Hera help me run Amazon advertising campaigns?

No. Hera's feature set is scheduling, compliance, fleet management and driver coaching for delivery contractors. It has no connection to Amazon's advertising console, DSP media buying, or ad measurement.

What's the real difference between the two things called DSP?

A Delivery Service Partner is a courier business that delivers Amazon packages using vans and drivers. A Demand-Side Platform is Amazon's ad-buying system for display, video and audio. They share three letters and nothing else — different businesses, different buyers, different software categories.

If last-click ROAS looks good, why isn't that enough proof Amazon DSP is working?

Last-click attribution credits whichever ad a shopper touched most recently, even if they were already going to convert. It can't separate ads that caused a sale from ads that simply happened to be nearby. Holdout tests and matched controls, reconciled in Amazon Marketing Cloud, are what actually isolate incremental lift.

Who should be looking at Amazon DSP advertising instead of Hera?

Brands that sell products on Amazon and want to reach shoppers with display, video or audio ads, on or off Amazon, need Amazon DSP — either run themselves, through an agency, or through a managed provider. If you don't sell a product on Amazon, DSP advertising isn't relevant to you at all.
