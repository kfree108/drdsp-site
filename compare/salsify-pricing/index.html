What the site says today

Checked 20 August 2026 on Salsify's own pages. The pricing page carries no tiers, no figures and no currency — it asks you to request a personalised demo instead. The platform page lists what is being sold: PIM, Syndication, Digital Shelf Analytics, Enhanced Content, an Intelligence Suite, a GDSN data pool and catalogue sites. No programmatic buying, no retail media activation, no Amazon DSP.

Which matters, because a lot of shortlists arrive here by mistake. DSP in advertising means Demand-Side Platform — the software that buys programmatic display, video and audio, including on Amazon. (It also, unhelpfully, means Delivery Service Partner in Amazon's logistics business; neither is Salsify.) How to read a quote for software that publishes no price

Quote-only is normal for enterprise product data platforms, because the work scales with catalogue complexity rather than with a seat count. That gives you the questions to ask, even without a rate card. Establish which dimensions drive the number:

  • Catalogue size — how many SKUs, and whether variants count individually.
  • Channel connections — how many retailers and marketplaces you syndicate to, and whether each new one is priced separately.
  • Seats and roles — who needs to edit versus who only needs to read.
  • Modules — which of PIM, syndication, analytics and enhanced content are in the base and which are add-ons.
  • Implementation — the one-off data-modelling and onboarding cost, which in this category is often material relative to year-one licence.
  • Term and uplift — the annual increase written into year two and three.

Then ask for the total three-year cost of ownership rather than the first-year price. Content platforms are sticky by design; the number that matters is the one you will be paying when moving away has become expensive.

The two budget lines, and why they get confused

Product content software and media buying are different purchases with different shapes, and finance teams conflate them constantly.

Content software is usually a licence: annual, banded by catalogue and connections, plus implementation. It is a fixed cost that makes every future marketing dollar work slightly better. Media is variable: the impressions themselves, plus a fee on top of or inside them. For comparison, in adjacent commerce media software Skai publishes annual licence bands on its own site — $114,000 a year for advertisers under four million dollars of annual spend when we read it on 20 August 2026 — while managed media is typically charged as a percentage of working media. Criteo's Commerce Max terms describe that shape precisely: a DSP Fee and, where applicable, a Managed Service Fee, both calculated as a percentage of working media spend, with the order-form budget inclusive of those fees.

Keep the two lines separate in the model. A licence does not scale with a campaign, and a media fee does not stop when you pause a flight.

Why a DSP company is telling you to fix the page first

Because buying reach into a page that does not convert is a way to fund Amazon, not your growth. Display's job is to create demand. If the demand arrives at a listing with thin content, missing attributes, weak imagery or a variation structure that hides the right size, you have paid the highest-cost traffic in the account to bounce.

What that waste looks like when it is measured properly: in one client account we are cleared to name, Walkize, our software read $267,131 of ad spend against $601,614 in attributed sales between December 2025 and August 2026 — and $89,885 of that spend, 33.6% of it, went to search terms that produced zero orders. 89,045 individual search terms took money and returned nothing, while the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of all sales. That is search rather than display, but the lesson transfers exactly: traffic bought without a mechanism for finding the dead third of it is not a growth strategy.

So the honest sequencing is: get the product data right, get the page converting, then buy reach — and measure the reach against a control rather than against a report that flatters it.

When content software is also the wrong purchase

We would be doing the same thing we criticise if we sent every reader to a demo. Product data platforms are excellent at a specific problem and expensive against a different one. Three cases where a smaller answer is the right one:

  • One marketplace, a few dozen SKUs. If Amazon is the only channel and the catalogue fits in a spreadsheet that one person maintains, an enterprise PIM is heavier than the problem. Fix the listings directly and spend the licence on media or on photography.
  • The bottleneck is conversion, not data completeness. Syndicating accurate attributes to nine retailers does not help if the main image, the first bullet and the price are what shoppers are rejecting. Test the page before you buy infrastructure for it.
  • Nobody owns the data model. These platforms repay a real internal owner. Bought without one, they become an expensive copy of the mess they were meant to fix, and the annual uplift arrives regardless.

Conversely, if you syndicate to many retailers, sell in several languages, or spend meaningful hours every week fixing attributes by hand, the licence usually pays for itself in avoided error — and it makes every subsequent media dollar land on a page that is ready for it.

Which one you should actually pick

Salsify suits brands with large catalogues syndicated across many retailers, where product data quality is the bottleneck and a licence is the right shape of cost. Dr. DSP suits Amazon-first brands whose pages already convert and whose open question is whether display is adding sales. They are sequential purchases, not competing ones.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Salsify cost?

No price is published; you request a demo. Expect the quote to be driven by catalogue size, the number of retailer connections, which modules you take and how many editing seats you need, with implementation priced separately. Ask for the three-year total, including the annual uplift, rather than the first-year figure.

Is Salsify a DSP?

No. Salsify is product experience and information management software — product data, syndication, enhanced content and digital shelf analytics. A demand-side platform buys advertising inventory. If you are shortlisting DSPs, Salsify is not on that list, though it may well belong in the same annual budget.

Should I buy content software before advertising?

If the listings are not converting, yes. Reach is the most expensive way to discover that a page does not work. A cheaper sequence is fixing the product data, confirming conversion on existing traffic, then buying display against a holdout so you can see what the reach actually added.

Can Salsify tell me whether my ads worked?

It can tell you about content quality and digital shelf presence, which are real inputs to advertising performance. It does not run advertising measurement. Proving that display added incremental sales requires a control group and a reconciliation of impression and conversion data — on Amazon, that happens in Amazon Marketing Cloud.
