Set the terms before the bake-off

Quick orientation. Acorn here is Acorn-i, the Amazon and retail media specialist now inside Jellyfish Commerce. DSP means Demand-Side Platform — programmatic buying of display, video and audio — and not Delivery Service Partner, the Amazon courier franchise that shares the acronym and confuses roughly a third of the searches in this space.

We are Dr. DSP, a managed Amazon DSP product from Full Circle, a full-service Amazon management company: $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands. We are on the scorecard below like everyone else, and we lose some of the rows on purpose.

The seven questions to score every candidate on

Give each candidate the same brief and score each question one to five. Do it before you look at price, because price without these answers is meaningless.

  • Custody. Who holds the Amazon DSP advertiser entity and the Amazon Marketing Cloud instance while we work together, and who holds them after?
  • Cadence. How often does a human or a system actually touch each line item — daily, weekly, or when something breaks?
  • Measurement. Will you design a holdout or matched control before the first flight, and reconcile in AMC rather than adding two platform reports together?
  • Fee architecture. Platform fee, service fee, data fee — which of these exist, at what rate, and is my budget inclusive of them?
  • Escalation. What is done without asking me, what is queued for approval, and can I move that line?
  • Exit. What do I take with me — raw line-item data, audiences, creative files, the change log?
  • Honesty. Under what circumstances would you tell me to stop buying display altogether?

The seventh question is the one that sorts the field fastest. Display is not a default purchase. If sponsored search still has profitable headroom, if the product page does not convert, or if inventory cannot absorb the demand, more reach makes things worse and faster.

Acorn-i vs Quartile — service depth against platform breadth

Different animals. Acorn-i sells expertise with software attached; Quartile sells a retail media optimisation platform with support attached, spanning Amazon sponsored ads and Amazon DSP alongside Walmart, Instacart, Criteo and the DTC channels. Quartile claims more than $2B in annual retail ad spend and 5,300-plus customers, and neither company publishes a price.

Quartile wins when you want automated bidding applied consistently across many retail networks, and reviewers on Capterra — 4.5 stars from 94 reviews when we checked on 20 August 2026 — consistently praise both the automation and the account teams. The recurring criticism is complexity: granular campaign structures that need technical fluency to manage. Acorn-i wins when the problem is judgment rather than throughput, especially on European retail media where their bench is deep. Our fuller read is on the Quartile review page.

Acorn-i vs Skai — the only one that shows its price

Skai is enterprise commerce media software with annual pricing published on its own site, banded by media volume: Skai from $114,000 a year at the entry band, and $276,000, $504,000 and $756,000 as annual media rises through ten, twenty and thirty-five million dollars — billed annually, reviewable after three months, read on 20 August 2026.

That transparency is worth something on its own — it lets you build a business case without a sales cycle. Skai suits organisations with an in-house trading function and governance requirements that favour a licence over a service. Acorn-i suits organisations that would rather buy the outcome than the console. The honest comparison is not the licence against a service fee; it is the licence plus a salaried operator against a service fee, and once you include the salary the gap narrows considerably.

Acorn-i vs Criteo Commerce Max — and the fee clause everyone should read

Criteo Commerce Max is a commerce DSP built for offsite retail media across many retailers, and it is unusually candid about how it charges — not in a pricing page, but in its Specific Terms of Service. Those terms state that fees are the Demand Side Platform Fee and, where applicable, a Managed Service Fee, both calculated as a percentage of Working Media Spend, and that the budget on the Order Form is inclusive of Service Fees. A Retailer Audience Data Fee can apply as a flat CPM or a fixed percentage on top.

Take that architecture into every other conversation on your shortlist, Acorn-i's included. It is a good structure, published plainly, and it gives you the exact vocabulary to ask anyone else the same questions. Criteo suits buyers whose retail media problem spans many retailers rather than concentrating on Amazon; Acorn-i suits buyers who want the strategy layer above the buying.

Acorn-i vs The Trade Desk — a comparison that is usually a category error

The Trade Desk describes itself as the leading independent demand-side platform, buy-side only, strongest in connected TV and the open internet, with identity and supply-path products like UID2 and OpenPath. It is genuinely excellent at what it does and it is not competing for the same job as an Amazon retail media specialist.

The distinction that matters: Amazon DSP buys against Amazon's own shopping signal and reconciles inside Amazon Marketing Cloud, where display impressions and sponsored-ads events meet at event level. An independent DSP buys the open internet extremely well. If your growth question is reach beyond Amazon, CTV, and audiences that have never met your brand, The Trade Desk belongs on the list — usually accessed through an agency, since it publishes no rate card and its /pricing URL returns a 404. We go deeper on the Trade Desk review and on how it prices.

Common questions

What does 'Acorn vs' usually mean in Amazon advertising?

Almost always Acorn-i measured against another retail media partner or platform. It is worth checking which Acorn you mean before comparing anything — the investing app and the streaming service dominate the search results, and none of the three are in the same business.

Can I compare an agency and a software platform fairly?

Only if you add the labour. Put a realistic salary for a competent trader into the software column, then compare totals. Also compare cadence honestly: a platform is available daily, but it is only used as often as someone logs in, and in most teams that is less often than planned.

Which comparison matters most if my spend is small?

The measurement one. Below a certain monthly budget, results are statistical noise and no holdout will reach significance, so you would be paying for a test you cannot act on. Ask each candidate what the minimum readable spend is for your category — a partner who answers honestly there will answer honestly everywhere else.

Should the winner be the one with the best case study?

No. Case studies are selected by definition. Ask for portfolio-level performance with the period attached, then ask what the worst quartile looked like and what they did about it. That answer tells you far more about how your account will be handled than the best month somebody ever had.
