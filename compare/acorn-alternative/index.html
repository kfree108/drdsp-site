Before the shortlist, two words that mislead people

DSP in this context is a Demand-Side Platform — the software used to buy programmatic display, video and audio, including on Amazon's own properties. It is not Delivery Service Partner, Amazon's van-and-driver franchise, which shares the initials and none of the meaning. And Acorn here is Acorn-i, the Amazon and retail media specialist now operating inside Jellyfish Commerce, not the consumer investing app of a similar name.

Who is writing: Dr. DSP, a managed Amazon DSP product from Full Circle, a full-service Amazon management company: $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands. We are one of the four categories below, and we will say clearly where the other three beat us.

Category one — another full-service commerce group

This is the like-for-like swap: a group that handles content, catalogue, retail media and creative across several retailers and several countries. Jellyfish Commerce itself is one, and there is a long bench of similar practices inside the large holding groups.

Who this genuinely suits. Brands selling on four or more retailers in three or more markets, where the hard problem is coordination rather than optimisation. If your Amazon spend is one of six lines in a European retail media budget and someone senior needs a single number for all of it, a group solves a problem a specialist cannot.

What to test. Seniority of the people actually assigned, not the people at the pitch; how programmatic buying is staffed relative to retail search; and whether display gets a genuine strategist or a shared trading desk. Groups are strongest at breadth and weakest at attention.

Category two — buy the platform and run it yourself

Replace the service with software and staff it internally. Skai is the most transparent option here: real annual prices on their own site, banded by media volume: Skai from $114,000 a year for advertisers under four million dollars of annual media, read on 20 August 2026. Quartile and Pacvue take demo requests rather than publishing rates. Criteo Commerce Max is a genuine commerce DSP with its fee architecture written into its terms of service.

Who this genuinely suits. Teams with a real in-house trader — someone whose job is the platform, not someone who checks it between meetings. If you have that person, software plus their salary usually beats a service fee, and you keep the institutional knowledge in the building.

What to test. Whether the platform buys Amazon DSP inventory directly or reports on it; who holds the DSP entity; and what happens to the account on the days your trader is on holiday. Software does not have a Tuesday.

Category three — a specialist Amazon DSP buyer

A team whose whole business is programmatic on Amazon, holding real seats and running them daily. Our own group's agency side, reMKTR, is exactly this, and it is the right answer for plenty of brands who do not want a product at all — they want media bought by people, on a service agreement, with a strategist to argue with.

Who this genuinely suits. Brands where Amazon is the main event, spend is large enough to justify dedicated attention, and the internal stakeholder wants a human on the call every week.

The number to ask any specialist for. Portfolio-level performance with the period attached. Ours, from a direct Amazon DSP API pull covering 30 advertisers in July 2026: 6.04x return on ad spend, $5.49 blended cost per acquisition across 57,137 attributed purchases, and 20.1% of those purchases from shoppers new to the brand. That is 30 advertisers in one month out of 109 live seats, on Amazon-attributed data — not a promise about your account, and not proof of incrementality on its own.

The handover checklist — this is what decides whether a switch hurts

Most switching pain is not strategic. It is custody. Ask these before you sign anything with a replacement, and before you give notice to the incumbent.

  • Who owns the DSP advertiser entity? If the seat belongs to the agency, your campaign history, your creative library and your bidding history may not travel.
  • Who owns the Amazon Marketing Cloud instance? The clean room holds the analysis that justified past decisions. Losing it means re-earning conclusions you already paid for.
  • What happens to audiences? Custom-built and modelled segments often live inside the incumbent's structure. Establish what can be rebuilt and how long the rebuild takes to become useful again.
  • Do you have raw reporting, not dashboards? Get flat exports at line-item level for at least the past twelve months before the relationship ends. Screenshots are not data.
  • Who holds the creative files? Not the rendered ads — the working files and the rights.
  • What is the notice period, and does it overlap? Two weeks of dual running costs less than a dark month in Q4.
  • What was actually changed in the last quarter, and why? If nobody can produce a change log with reasoning, that is the strongest possible argument for whichever replacement writes one.

Run that list first. Then pick. A worse partner with a clean handover often outperforms a better one you cannot migrate to.

Common questions

Is there a direct like-for-like replacement for Acorn-i?

The closest match is another full-service commerce group with both content and retail media capability, because that combination is what Acorn-i grew into. If you only ever used them for Amazon advertising, you are buying a much narrower thing than you are replacing, and a specialist or a managed product will usually give that narrow thing more attention.

How long does it take to move an Amazon DSP account?

The mechanical part is fast; the useful part is not. Budget for the audiences and optimisation history to take weeks to become as good as they were, and plan the switch away from peak season. Overlap the notice period so somebody is watching pacing during the transition rather than nobody.

Can I keep my Amazon Marketing Cloud data when I switch?

Depends entirely on who owns the instance and how it was set up, which is why it is on the checklist above. Establish it in writing before you give notice. If you cannot keep it, get raw exports of the analyses that informed decisions, so the new partner is arguing from evidence rather than starting from scratch.

Should I run two partners at once during the change?

For a short, defined overlap, yes — it protects pacing and gives you a clean before-and-after. Do not run two partners on the same line items indefinitely: you will get two reports claiming the same conversions, which is the same double-counting problem that makes last-click unreliable in the first place.

What if I decide display was never the right spend?

That is a legitimate outcome and worth designing for. Ask each candidate what evidence would make them recommend cutting DSP entirely, and how they would test it. A partner who has an answer to that has thought about your business; a partner who has never considered it is selling a line item.
