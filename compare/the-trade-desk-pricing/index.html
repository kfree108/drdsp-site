What we checked, and what it returned

The structure, which is more durable than any number

The Trade Desk's commercial model is well established even where the rate is not public: a platform fee expressed as a percentage of media spend, with other costs passed through and itemised. What sits alongside it on a real invoice:

  • Data fees. Third-party audience segments carry their own CPM or percentage cost, set by the data provider rather than the platform.
  • Measurement and verification. Viewability, brand safety and attribution vendors bill separately.
  • Supply-path costs. What the exchange and the publisher take before your money becomes an impression.
  • Agency or trading fee. If you access the platform through an agency — which most brands do — there is a fee for the humans operating it.

Model all five. A buyer who negotiates the platform fee hard and ignores the other four has optimised the smallest line on the page.

How to verify a take rate yourself, instead of trusting a listicle

Search results for this keyword are full of confident percentages. Most trace back to each other rather than to a source. Here is a method that does not require trusting anyone, including us.

The Trade Desk is a public company. Its own investor materials report revenue, and the company discusses the gross value of media transacted on its platform. Divide reported revenue by gross spend for the same period and you have a blended take rate, computed from primary disclosure. Do it for the most recent full year on investors.thetradedesk.com and you will have a defensible figure with a date attached — which is more than any blog post will give you, and it will still be right next quarter when you refresh it.

Two caveats worth carrying into the negotiation. A blended take rate across every advertiser on a platform is not the rate you will be offered; large committed volume prices differently from small flexible volume. And a blended rate includes product mix — data, CTV and identity products priced differently from standard display — so your invoice will not decompose the way the average does.

What one point costs, in money rather than percentages

The reason to do this arithmetic before the call, not after the invoice:

  • Media at, say, $100,000 a month: one percentage point of fee, $1,000 a month — $12,000 a year.
  • Five points, $60,000 a year, which for most brands is the difference between running a fourth market or not.
  • If the fee is deducted from the media budget rather than added to it — which is exactly how Criteo's Commerce Max terms describe their own model, where the order-form budget is inclusive of Service Fees — then each point costs you twice: you pay it, and you lose a point of working media that would have bought impressions.

Where an Amazon-first brand should actually be looking

If your business runs mostly on Amazon, the more important question is not what The Trade Desk charges. It is which platform can prove the display worked.

Last-click attribution cannot prove incrementality and never could. It tells you which touch happened last. On Amazon, where a shopper sees display and then searches your brand name before buying, that systematically hands credit to whichever surface stood nearest the checkout. Two reports, one order, one business — added together they describe a company larger than the one in your bank account.

What answers it is a holdout — withhold display from a matched region or ASIN set and compare — reconciled inside Amazon Marketing Cloud, Amazon's clean room, where DSP impressions and sponsored-ads events meet at event level and stop double-counting each other. That capability is native to buying through Amazon DSP. It is the structural reason an Amazon-first brand starts there rather than with an open-internet platform, however good the open-internet platform is — and The Trade Desk is very good at the thing it is for.

For scale, our own book, pulled from the Amazon DSP API and scoped exactly as pulled — 30 advertisers in July 2026, not all 109 seats we run: 78.4 million impressions at a $4.00 CPM, a blended $1.42 cost per click, $5.49 cost per acquisition across 57,137 attributed purchases, 6.04x return on ad spend and 20.1% new-to-brand. Amazon-attributed figures, which show efficient buying and not, on their own, incrementality. No guarantee attaches to them.

What to ask for in writing, from anyone

  • The platform fee percentage, in the contract, with the volume tier that produced it.
  • Whether the fee is inclusive or on top of the media budget you approve.
  • Every pass-through named individually: data, measurement, verification, supply-path.
  • The minimum commitment and the term — quarterly, annual, or spend-based.
  • Who owns the seat if an agency is operating the platform on your behalf.
  • What the reporting deliverable is, and whether it includes a measurement design or only delivery metrics.

Which one you should actually pick

The Trade Desk suits brands and agencies buying open-internet and CTV reach at scale, where its independence and supply-path work genuinely matter, and where a negotiated platform fee against large volume is normal. Amazon-first brands should weigh a different thing entirely: whether the platform lets them prove incrementality in AMC. That is the ground Dr. DSP is built on.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does The Trade Desk cost?

They do not publish it, and the pricing URL on their own site is a 404. The model is a platform fee charged as a percentage of media, agreed in your contract, with data, measurement and supply-path costs itemised separately. To get a grounded figure, ask for the percentage in writing with the volume tier attached.

Is there a minimum spend for The Trade Desk?

No minimum is published on their site, and figures circulating online are unsourced. Most brands access the platform through an agency, which changes the commercial shape entirely. Ask the specific question — minimum commitment, term length, and whether it is quarterly or annual — and get it in the contract rather than the deck.

Is The Trade Desk cheaper than Amazon DSP?

Not a comparison you can settle on fees, because they buy different inventory against different signals. The right question is which platform can show that the spend produced incremental sales for your business. If most of your revenue is on Amazon, the reconciliation you need happens in Amazon Marketing Cloud.

Why do so many sources quote a percentage for The Trade Desk?

Because a blended take rate can be derived from public financial disclosure — revenue against gross media transacted. That derived figure is real but it is an average across every advertiser and product on the platform, not an offer. Compute it yourself from investor materials, date it, and treat it as context rather than a quote.
